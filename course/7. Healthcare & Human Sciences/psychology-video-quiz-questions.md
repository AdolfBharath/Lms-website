## Module 1: Psychology Foundations and Human Behaviour

1. What is the main purpose of studying psychology?
   - A. To understand behaviour, thoughts, emotions, and mental processes
   - B. To predict every action with complete certainty
   - C. To replace medical diagnosis
   - D. To avoid studying social behaviour
   - Answer: A

2. Which example best reflects psychological observation?
   - A. Noticing how sleep, stress, and environment affect a person's mood
   - B. Guessing a person's character from one conversation
   - C. Ignoring behaviour and focusing only on appearance
   - D. Assuming all people react the same way
   - Answer: A

3. Why is scientific thinking important in psychology?
   - A. It helps separate evidence-based conclusions from assumptions
   - B. It removes the need to understand people
   - C. It makes personal opinion stronger than data
   - D. It avoids careful observation
   - Answer: A

4. In introductory psychology, behaviour is best understood as:
   - A. Actions influenced by mental, biological, social, and environmental factors
   - B. Random actions with no pattern
   - C. Only physical movement
   - D. Something unrelated to emotions
   - Answer: A

5. Which statement best describes mental processes?
   - A. Internal activities such as thinking, memory, emotion, and decision-making
   - B. Only visible actions
   - C. Only physical health symptoms
   - D. Events that cannot be studied
   - Answer: A

6. What is a useful first step when analysing human behaviour?
   - A. Understand the situation, context, and possible influences
   - B. Label the person immediately
   - C. Ignore emotional state
   - D. Focus only on one cause
   - Answer: A

7. Why should psychology learners avoid overgeneralising?
   - A. People differ across background, experience, biology, and environment
   - B. Everyone has identical mental patterns
   - C. One example proves a universal rule
   - D. Context is never important
   - Answer: A

8. Which skill is most useful for a beginner in psychology?
   - A. Careful listening and evidence-based interpretation
   - B. Quick judgement
   - C. Ignoring non-verbal signs
   - D. Memorising labels without meaning
   - Answer: A

9. Why is psychology relevant to daily life?
   - A. It helps explain learning, relationships, motivation, stress, and choices
   - B. It is useful only in hospitals
   - C. It applies only to abnormal behaviour
   - D. It avoids real-life problems
   - Answer: A

10. What does the program introduction usually help learners understand?
    - A. Course structure, expectations, and learning path
    - B. Final answers to every assessment
    - C. Reasons to skip foundational topics
    - D. How to avoid practice
    - Answer: A

11. A healthy approach to psychology learning includes:
    - A. Curiosity, ethical awareness, and respect for individual differences
    - B. Labelling people casually
    - C. Treating one theory as always perfect
    - D. Avoiding reflection
    - Answer: A

12. What makes psychology different from casual advice?
    - A. It uses structured concepts, evidence, and careful interpretation
    - B. It relies only on personal opinion
    - C. It ignores research
    - D. It discourages questioning
    - Answer: A

13. Which factor can influence mental health and behaviour?
    - A. Sleep, relationships, stress, environment, and biology
    - B. Only personality
    - C. Only age
    - D. Only academic performance
    - Answer: A

14. Which statement is most accurate?
    - A. Psychology studies both typical and atypical patterns of behaviour
    - B. Psychology studies only disorders
    - C. Psychology has no connection to society
    - D. Psychology avoids emotions
    - Answer: A

15. The best learning outcome from an introduction to psychology is:
    - A. A clear foundation for understanding people with empathy and evidence
    - B. A fixed label for every person
    - C. A list of memorised terms with no application
    - D. Avoiding complex behaviour
    - Answer: A

## Module 2: Mental Health, Sleep, Thinking Patterns, and Regulation

1. Why is sleep important for mental health?
   - A. It supports mood regulation, memory, attention, and recovery
   - B. It has no effect on mental health
   - C. It only affects physical growth
   - D. It replaces emotional support
   - Answer: A

2. Poor sleep can commonly affect students by increasing:
   - A. Irritability, poor focus, stress, and emotional sensitivity
   - B. Perfect concentration
   - C. Guaranteed motivation
   - D. Complete immunity from anxiety
   - Answer: A

3. What are cognitive distortions?
   - A. Unhelpful thinking patterns that can make situations feel worse than they are
   - B. Physical injuries to the brain only
   - C. Always accurate conclusions
   - D. A type of sleep stage
   - Answer: A

4. Which is an example of all-or-nothing thinking?
   - A. "If I fail one test, I am a complete failure."
   - B. "I can improve with practice."
   - C. "One result does not define me."
   - D. "I should review my mistakes."
   - Answer: A

5. How can cognitive distortions affect student mental health?
   - A. They can increase stress, self-doubt, avoidance, and emotional distress
   - B. They always improve confidence
   - C. They remove the need for support
   - D. They make learning effortless
   - Answer: A

6. What is emotional regulation?
   - A. The ability to notice, understand, and manage emotional responses
   - B. Never feeling emotions
   - C. Suppressing every feeling permanently
   - D. Reacting immediately without reflection
   - Answer: A

7. Which strategy supports emotional regulation?
   - A. Pausing, naming the emotion, breathing, and choosing a response
   - B. Ignoring emotions until they explode
   - C. Blaming others automatically
   - D. Avoiding every difficult situation
   - Answer: A

8. Anxiety is best described as:
   - A. A state of worry, tension, or fear about possible threats or outcomes
   - B. A sign that a person is weak
   - C. Always useful and never harmful
   - D. The same as happiness
   - Answer: A

9. Overthinking often becomes harmful when it:
   - A. Repeats worries without leading to useful action
   - B. Helps clarify a realistic next step
   - C. Encourages problem-solving
   - D. Supports planning
   - Answer: A

10. Which approach can help with overthinking?
    - A. Identify the thought, test evidence, and choose one next action
    - B. Keep replaying the same worry
    - C. Avoid all responsibilities
    - D. Treat every thought as a fact
    - Answer: A

11. Psychosomatic disorders involve:
    - A. Physical symptoms influenced by psychological stress or emotional factors
    - B. Imaginary problems that should be ignored
    - C. Only infectious disease
    - D. Colour preference only
    - Answer: A

12. Why should psychosomatic symptoms be handled carefully?
    - A. The distress is real and may need both medical and psychological support
    - B. The person is pretending
    - C. Symptoms should never be checked
    - D. Only discipline is needed
    - Answer: A

13. Which habit supports better mental health in students?
    - A. Sleep routine, support-seeking, realistic thinking, and emotion regulation
    - B. Isolation and constant self-criticism
    - C. Avoiding sleep
    - D. Ignoring stress signs
    - Answer: A

14. A helpful response to emotional distress is:
    - A. Recognise the feeling and use a safe coping strategy
    - B. Shame the person for feeling upset
    - C. Tell them to stop thinking forever
    - D. Compare their pain with someone else's
    - Answer: A

15. What is the main link between sleep, thoughts, and emotions?
    - A. Each can influence the others and affect mental wellbeing
    - B. They are completely unrelated
    - C. Only thoughts matter
    - D. Only sleep matters
    - Answer: A

## Module 3: Disorders, Neurodevelopment, Risk, and Support

   - A. A neurodevelopmental condition involving differences in communication, behaviour, and sensory processing
   - B. A temporary mood problem
   - C. A lack of intelligence
   - D. A behaviour chosen for attention
   - Answer: A

   - B. Every autistic person is exactly the same
   - C. It only affects one age group
   - D. It has no variation
   - Answer: A

3. ADHD commonly involves difficulty with:
   - A. Attention regulation, impulse control, and activity level
   - B. Moral character
   - C. Vision only
   - D. Sleep only
   - Answer: A

4. A supportive approach for ADHD includes:
   - A. Structure, reminders, realistic goals, and reduced shame
   - B. Punishment without understanding
   - C. Ignoring all symptoms
   - D. Assuming laziness
   - Answer: A

5. Psychosis may include:
   - A. Hallucinations, delusions, or difficulty distinguishing reality
   - B. Better memory only
   - C. A normal study habit
   - D. A marketing tool
   - Answer: A

   - B. It is always harmless
   - C. It should be mocked
   - D. It never affects behaviour

7. Personality disorders generally involve:
   - A. Long-term patterns in thinking, feeling, relating, and behaving
   - B. A single bad mood

## Module 5: Wellbeing, Risk, and Support

1. Why is wellbeing important in psychology?
   - A. It helps learners understand how emotional, social, and behavioural health affect daily functioning
   - B. It only focuses on physical fitness
   - C. It ignores mental health risks
   - D. It replaces professional care
   - Answer: A

2. Which topic is most connected to wellbeing support?
   - A. Recognising distress signs and helping the person access appropriate support
   - B. Ignoring changes in behaviour
   - C. Blaming the person for symptoms
   - D. Avoiding all conversations about mental health
   - Answer: A

3. Substance abuse becomes a risk when it:
   - A. Harms health, relationships, judgement, responsibilities, or safety
   - B. Improves every part of functioning
   - C. Has no effect on behaviour
   - D. Automatically solves stress
   - Answer: A

4. Which response is most helpful when substance abuse is suspected?
   - A. Approach with concern, avoid judgement, and encourage safe professional help
   - B. Publicly shame the person
   - C. Ignore warning signs
   - D. Force advice without listening

5. Attachments influence wellbeing because they affect:
   - A. Trust, emotional security, relationship patterns, and support-seeking
   - B. Only academic marks
   - C. Only colour preference
   - D. Only criminal behaviour
   - Answer: A

6. Healthy boundaries support mental health by:
   - A. Clarifying limits, protecting emotional energy, and improving respectful communication
   - B. Ending every relationship
   - C. Allowing one person to control another
   - D. Preventing honest communication
   - Answer: A

7. Psychosomatic symptoms should be taken seriously because:
   - A. Psychological distress can be linked with real physical discomfort and needs careful support
   - B. The person is always pretending
   - C. Physical symptoms never need attention

    - B. Discuss personal details publicly
    - C. Use labels casually
    - D. Treat serious risk as entertainment

15. The main purpose of a wellbeing and support module is to:
    - A. Help learners recognise risk, reduce stigma, and respond with safe support pathways
    - B. Teach learners to diagnose everyone
    - C. Replace medical and psychological care
